---
title: Channels
description: Email, in-app and push, web, iOS, macOS, Android and Windows. A channel is on when the config has an entry for it.
---

A channel is on when `channels` in the config has an entry for it, and each type’s template turns
it on for that type. The **Channels** page in the dashboard shows what Chaski picked up from your
config.

## Email

Email goes out through Cloudflare Email Service, from a domain you onboarded to it, through the
`send_email` binding you pass as `bindings.email`.

```ts
channels: {
  email: {
    from: 'notifications@example.com',
    fromName: 'My App',
    replyTo: 'support@example.com',
  },
},
```

`from` must be an address the binding allows. A send can override `fromName` and `replyTo`, and
attach up to 10 files when `bindings.attachments` holds an R2 bucket. The HTML of every email carries a
pixel served from your mount, which marks the delivery opened when the message is read; that’s
why `publicUrl` must be the absolute URL of the dashboard.

A contact without an email address skips the channel with `missing_email`.

## In-app

The in-app channel keeps a feed per contact in the `ChaskiFeed` Durable Object, and pushes each new
item to their open pages over a WebSocket.

```ts
channels: {
  in_app: {},
},
```

It has no options yet: `{}` turns it on, with `bindings.feed` set. Your frontend reads the feed
through `chaski.userRoutes()`, as [Browser clients](/docs/browser-clients) shows; on the server,
`chaski.feed` lists it, counts the unread items and marks them read.

## Push

Push reaches browsers through web push, Apple devices through APNs, Android devices through FCM
and Windows devices through WNS. Configure any of them:

```ts
channels: {
  push: {
    vapid: {
      subject: 'mailto:ops@example.com',
      publicKey: env.VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY,
      privateKey: env.VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY,
    },
    apns: {
      teamId: '<Apple team id>',
      keyId: '<APNs key id>',
      privateKey: env.APNS_PRIVATE_KEY,
      ios: { bundleId: 'com.example.app', environment: 'production' },
      macos: { bundleId: 'com.example.desktop', environment: 'production' },
    },
    fcm: {
      serviceAccount: env.FCM_SERVICE_ACCOUNT,
    },
    wns: {
      tenantId: '<directory (tenant) id>',
      clientId: '<application (client) id>',
      clientSecret: env.WNS_CLIENT_SECRET,
    },
  },
},
```

- `vapid` takes the key pair web-push tools print: the base64url public key and the private key,
  or the whole private JWK as JSON. `subject` is a `mailto:` or `https:` contact the push services
  can reach you at.
- `apns` takes your team id, the key’s id and the contents of the `.p8` file, which send to every
  app of your team. `ios` and `macos` each name an app: its bundle id and the APNs environment of
  its tokens, `sandbox` for development builds and `production` for the rest.
- `fcm` takes the contents of a service account’s JSON key file, from **Project settings → Service
  accounts** in the Firebase console. The project it names must be the one your Android app
  registers its tokens with.
- `wns` takes the Microsoft Entra app registration your Windows app creates its push channel with,
  through the Windows App SDK’s `PushNotificationManager`: its tenant id, its client id and one of
  its client secrets.

A contact receives push on the devices registered to them. Browsers register with
`client.push.enable()` from `chaskijs/client`; your app can register an iOS or macOS device token,
an Android FCM token or a Windows channel URI from the server:

```ts
await chaski.push.register({
  recipient: { id: user.id },
  platform: 'android', // or 'ios', 'macos', 'windows'
  deviceToken,
});
```

An Apple token belongs to the environment of the build that made it: a development build’s token
only works in `sandbox`. Pass `environment: 'sandbox'` when that build registers, and the app’s
environment in the config applies to every other device. When APNs refuses a token as
`BadDeviceToken` or `DeviceTokenNotForTopic`, Chaski forgets the device and hands the reason to
[`onError`](/docs/configuration#onerror): the token may be fine and the environment or the bundle
id wrong.

An Electron app on macOS gets its token from `pushNotifications.registerForAPNSNotifications()`
in the main process, and registers it with `client.push.registerDevice({ platform: 'macos',
deviceToken })`, as [Browser clients](/docs/browser-clients) shows.

On Windows, the device token is the channel URI WNS hands the app. Chaski only accepts one on
`notify.windows.com`, since it sends its access token there. The push arrives as a toast whose
`launch` arguments carry the `href` and `tag`, which the Windows App SDK hands the app as
`AppNotificationActivatedEventArgs.Arguments` when the user clicks it. WNS channels expire after
30 days, so the app registers its channel again on every launch; Chaski forgets one WNS answers
404 or 410 for.

A send with `push: { silent: true }` shows its alert without a sound, on Apple devices, on Windows
and in browsers. On Android, the notification channel your app creates decides the sound.

On Android the notification shows on its own when the app is in the background; the `href` and
`tag` of the push arrive in the intent’s extras when the user taps it.

A contact without a registered device skips the channel with `no_device_tokens`.

<Callout title="Subscriptions belong to the key they were made with.">
  Keep your VAPID key pair once browsers subscribe: a subscription made with another public key can
  never receive this server’s pushes, and the client subscribes again when it sees one.
</Callout>
