title: Channels
description: Email, in-app and push, web, iOS, macOS, Android and Windows. Each channel is set up from the dashboard, and is on once it has settings.
---

Every channel starts off. It turns on when you save its settings on the **Channels** page of the
dashboard, and each type’s template turns it on for that type. Removing a channel’s settings turns
it off again: its sends skip it as `channel_not_configured`, and the deliveries it still had queued
fail with that reason. A pause is something else: it holds a channel’s deliveries until you resume
it; see [Pause a channel](/docs/dashboard#pause-a-channel).

Settings are saved only through the dashboard, so a new environment sends nothing, not even in-app,
until Cloudflare Access covers its URL and someone saves its channels; under `wrangler dev`, the
identity under `access.dev` signs you in. Each environment’s Chaski keeps settings of its own, and
the push credentials among them are encrypted with `CHASKI_SECRET`. A setting that stops working, such as credentials saved under
another `CHASKI_SECRET`, shows its channel as **Failing** until you save it again.
[Configuration](/docs/configuration#channel-settings) lists every field.

## Email

Email goes out through Cloudflare Email Service, from a domain you onboarded to it, through the
`CHASKI_EMAIL` binding. Set its sender address, and optionally a sender name and a reply-to
address. The address must be one the binding allows. The page says when the binding is
missing.

A send can override the sender name and the reply-to address, and attach up to 10 files when
`CHASKI_ATTACHMENTS` holds an R2 bucket. The HTML of every email carries a pixel, served through
your app’s `userRoutes()`, which marks the delivery opened when the message is read; set
`CHASKI_PUBLIC_URL` to that mount, and without it emails carry no pixel.

A contact without an email address skips the channel with `missing_email`.

## In-app

The in-app channel keeps a feed per contact in the `ChaskiFeed` Durable Object, and pushes each new
item to their open pages over a WebSocket. It has no settings: turn it on, with `CHASKI_FEED`
bound. Your frontend reads the feed through your app’s `userRoutes()`, as
[Browser clients](/docs/browser-clients) shows; on the server, `env.CHASKI.feed()` lists it, counts
the unread items and marks them read.

## Push

Push reaches browsers through web push, Apple devices through APNs, Android devices through FCM
and Windows devices through WNS. Set up any of them; push is on once one is.

- **Web push** takes a contact, a `mailto:` or `https:` address the push services can reach you
  at, and a key pair: **Generate keys** makes one, and **Use existing keys** takes the pair you
  already send with.
- **APNs** takes your team id, the key’s id and the `.p8` file, which send to every app of your
  team. The iOS app and the macOS app each take their bundle ID and the APNs environment of their
  tokens: Sandbox for development builds, Production for TestFlight and App Store builds.
- **FCM** takes a service account’s JSON key file, from **Project settings → Service accounts** in
  the Firebase console. The project it names must be the one your Android app registers its tokens
  with.
- **WNS** takes the Microsoft Entra app registration your Windows app creates its push channel
  with, through the Windows App SDK’s `PushNotificationManager`: its tenant id, its client id and
  one of its client secrets.

A contact receives push on the devices registered to them. Browsers register with
`client.push.enable()` from `chaskijs/client`; your app can register an iOS or macOS device token,
an Android FCM token or a Windows channel URI from the server:

```ts
await env.CHASKI.push().register({
  recipient: { id: user.id },
  platform: 'android', // or 'ios', 'macos', 'windows'
  deviceToken,
});
```

A mobile app can also register its own token through your app’s `userRoutes()`, with
`client.push.registerDevice()`, as the signed-in user.

An Apple token belongs to the environment of the build that made it: a development build’s token
only works in `sandbox`. Pass `environment: 'sandbox'` when that build registers, and the app’s
environment in the settings applies to every other device. When APNs refuses a token as
`BadDeviceToken` or `DeviceTokenNotForTopic`, Chaski forgets the device and logs the reason to
Workers Logs: the token may be fine and the environment or the bundle id wrong.

An Electron app on macOS gets its token from `pushNotifications.registerForAPNSNotifications()`
in the main process, and registers it with `client.push.registerDevice({ platform: 'macos',
deviceToken })`, as [Browser clients](/docs/browser-clients) shows.

On Windows, the device token is the channel URI WNS hands the app. Chaski only accepts one on
`notify.windows.com`, since it sends its access token there. The push arrives as a toast whose
`launch` arguments carry the `href` and `tag`, which the Windows App SDK hands the app as
`AppNotificationActivatedEventArgs.Arguments` when the user clicks it. WNS channels expire after
30 days, so the app registers its channel again on every launch; Chaski forgets one WNS answers
404 or 410 for.

A send with `push: { silent: true }` shows its alert without a sound, on Apple devices, on Windows
and in browsers. On Android, the notification channel your app creates decides the sound.

Every push carries the title, the body, the link (`href`) and a `tag`: the send’s
`push.collapseId`, or the notification id, which the [feed item](/docs/api#feed-items) also
carries as `notificationId`. A push with the tag of one already shown replaces it, on Apple
devices, Android and in browsers. The send’s `data` comes along as is:

| Platform | Where `href`, `tag` and `data` arrive |
| --- | --- |
| Web push | The push’s JSON: `{ title, body, href, tag, silent, data }`. |
| iOS and macOS | Custom keys next to `aps`, one per `data` key. |
| Android | The message’s `data`, one per `data` key. |
| Windows | The toast’s launch arguments, one per `data` key. |

Give your app’s own notification of the same event the same id, `collapseId` or the notification
id, and the system shows one of the two. An app that shows the feed’s items itself while it runs
can also take its device out of push while its feed is open; see
[Browser clients](/docs/browser-clients#while-the-app-runs).

On Android the notification shows on its own when the app is in the background; the `href` and
`tag` of the push arrive in the intent’s extras when the user taps it.

A contact without a registered device skips the channel with `no_device_tokens`.

<Callout title="Subscriptions belong to the key they were made with.">
  Keep your web push key pair once browsers subscribe: a subscription made with another public key
  can never receive this Chaski’s pushes. The dashboard asks before **Replace keys** swaps the pair,
  and each browser’s client subscribes again the next time it sees the new key.
</Callout>
